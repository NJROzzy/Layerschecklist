Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

Before any model, you need to actually understand your data — its shape, its quality, its quirks. EDA is that first look.

Interview mindset:

For every dataset: What does each column mean? What's missing? What's the distribution? What relationships jump out?

Summary Statistics

Quick numeric overview of every column — mean, std, min, max, quartiles — before looking at a single row in detail.

Interview check: Know what a large gap between mean and median suggests about skewness. 

import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_csv("data.csv")
df.describe()
Missing Values

Almost every real dataset has gaps. How you handle them (drop, impute, flag) affects everything downstream.

Interview check: Know the tradeoff between dropping rows and imputing missing values. 

df.isnull().sum()

df["age"].fillna(df["age"].median(), inplace=True)
Distributions

Visualizing how values in a column are spread reveals skew, multimodal patterns, and potential outliers.

Interview check: Know when a histogram vs. a KDE plot is more appropriate. 

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

df["age"].hist(bins=20)
plt.show()
Correlation

Measures how strongly two numeric variables move together — useful for spotting redundant features or potential predictors.

Interview check: Know that correlation doesn't imply causation, and that it only captures linear relationships. 

corr = df.corr()

import seaborn as sns
sns.heatmap(corr, annot=True)

EDA Interview Questions You Should Be Able to Answer

  • What's the first thing you'd check when given a new dataset?
  • How would you decide whether to drop or impute missing values?
  • What does a skewed distribution tell you?
  • Why doesn't correlation imply causation?
  • What's the difference between a histogram and a boxplot, and when would you use each?

The Standard You Want

You should be able to look at a new dataset and, within a few minutes, describe its shape, flag its problems, and point to what looks worth investigating further.

Good models start with genuinely understanding the data, not skipping to the fun part.
