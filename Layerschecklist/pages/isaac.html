Isaac (NVIDIA Isaac Sim)

Isaac Sim is NVIDIA's robotics simulation platform — built to run many physics simulations in parallel on a GPU, making it well suited for training reinforcement learning policies at scale.

Interview mindset:

For every setup: How many environments are running in parallel? What's actually different between simulation and the real robot?

GPU-Parallel Environments

Instead of running one simulation at a time, Isaac Sim can run thousands of copies simultaneously on a GPU — massively speeding up RL training.

Interview check: Know why this matters for reinforcement learning specifically: RL needs huge amounts of experience to train well. 

# Conceptual — thousands of environments
# stepped together on GPU
envs = IsaacEnv(num_envs=4096)
obs = envs.reset()
Domain Randomization

Randomizing simulation parameters (friction, lighting, mass) during training so the trained policy generalizes better to the real world.

Interview check: Know why this directly addresses the "sim-to-real gap." 

# Conceptual
env.randomize_physics(friction_range=(0.5, 1.5))
Sim-to-Real Transfer

The process of taking a policy trained entirely in simulation and deploying it on physical hardware — never a perfect 1:1 match.

Interview check: Be able to name at least one technique used to close the sim-to-real gap (domain randomization, fine-tuning on real data). 

# Trained in sim, deployed on hardware
policy.load("trained_in_sim.pt")
real_robot.run(policy)
